# Paywall research (R37, 21 October 2026)

What comparable kids/learning apps (Speech Blubs, Lingokids, Pok Pok, Duolingo, Calm/Headspace) and the paywall-tool vendors' reviews have in common:

1. **Centred, outcome-first layout:** a large hero picture, one strong headline, then 3 short benefit lines with check marks. They do not use a grid of small icons.
2. **Plans as 2–3 full-width rows:** the yearly plan is preselected and carries a badge ("Best value" or "% off") plus its per-month price. Monthly sits under it, and a lifetime option only where the app sells one.
3. **The trial is not a separate choice.** One primary button carries it ("Start your free week" / "Start 7-day free trial"). A short line under the button says what happens after the trial. Adapty's Speech Blubs review recommends exactly this, rather than repeating the trial wording elsewhere on the screen.
4. **One primary call to action:** a visible close (X), with small links for Restore, Terms and Privacy.
5. **Shown on Day 0, at the end of onboarding.** RevenueCat reports that most trial starts and paid conversions happen on the install day.
6. **Kids apps speak to the parent, not the child.** Purchases sit behind a parent gate (Pok Pok case study).

Our trial is app-side (Google sign-in, no payment details). After 7 days the child goes back to the free games. So the button reads "Start 7-day free trial" while the trial is unused, and "Continue – {price}" afterwards.
